# ☕ Coffee Delivery App — UI/UX Redesign

A complete redesign focused on creating a clearer, more engaging, and visually consistent coffee-ordering experience.

Duration: 3 Months

Role: UI/UX Designer

Platform: Mobile

Tool: Figma

Resources: YouTube, ChatGPT, Pinterest

---

01. Overview

This project is a redesign of my first Coffee Delivery App, which I originally created as a beginner in UI/UX.
After gaining more knowledge and experience, I revisited the original design and realized there were several areas that could be improved — from layout and spacing to navigation, visual hierarchy, user flows, and overall consistency.
Instead of making small changes, I decided to redesign the application from the ground up.
The goal was to create a more thoughtful and polished experience while learning how to make better design decisions based on user needs.

---

02. The Background

My first coffee app was created within one month.
At that stage, I was mainly curious about UI/UX and wanted to learn the basics. I focused heavily on creating the final screens and had very little interest in sketching, wireframing, or understanding the complete UX process.
That project helped me discover that UI/UX was much more than simply creating visually appealing screens.
This became the motivation behind the redesign.
This time, I wanted to understand and follow the process instead of jumping directly into the final UI.

---

03. Design Process

I followed a more structured process this time:

📱 Sketching -
I started by sketching ideas and layouts on my mobile to explore different possibilities quickly.

🖥️ Wireframing -
I converted the sketches into wireframes in Figma to establish the structure, hierarchy, navigation, and content placement before focusing on visuals.

🎨 UI Design -
Once the structure was refined, I developed the high-fidelity UI with a warm, coffee-inspired visual style.

🔄 Interactive Prototyping -
Finally, I connected the screens and created interactive states using Figma components, variants, variables, and conditional interactions.

Sketch → Wireframe → UI Design → Interactive Prototype

---

04. Problems Identified in the Previous Design

Reviewing my old project helped me identify several design problems.

📐 Inconsistent spacing and alignment -
Different screens used inconsistent spacing, margins, and component positioning.

🔤 Weak visual hierarchy -
Important information did not always stand out clearly, making some screens harder to scan.

🧩 Inconsistent components -
Similar elements were not always designed consistently across different screens.

🧠 Limited UX thinking -
I had focused more on how the screens looked rather than why certain elements and interactions were necessary.

🛒 Basic interaction experience -
The earlier prototype had limited dynamic interactions and did not always behave like a real application.

📱 Direct jump to UI -
I had previously skipped much of the sketching and wireframing process, which made it harder to identify structural problems early.

---

05. Problems I Wanted to Solve

The redesign focused on answering a few important questions:

How can users discover coffee more easily?

How can customization be made simple and clear?

How can the ordering process feel straightforward?

How can offers be presented without overwhelming users?

How can the interface remain visually consistent across many screens?

How can the prototype feel more realistic without adding unnecessary complexity?

---

06. Solutions

☕ Simplified Coffee Discovery

I structured the home experience around:
* Search
* Categories
* Offers
* Recommendations


This allows users to quickly discover products without navigating through unnecessary screens.

🎨 Improved Visual Hierarchy

I used typography, spacing, size, and placement more intentionally to create a clear information hierarchy.

Important actions and information are given more visual emphasis.

🧩 Consistent Components

Reusable components and variants were used for repeated UI elements.

This helped maintain consistency and made the design easier to update.

☕ Clear Product Customization

The customization experience was designed to make choices such as:

* Size
* Milk
* Other available options

easy to understand before adding an item to the cart.

🛒 More Realistic Cart

I used Figma variables to create dynamic cart behaviour.

Items can appear when added, quantities can change, and the cart state responds to user actions.

❤️ Interactive Wishlist

The wishlist was designed with interactive states so users can add and remove products using the heart interaction.

🎟️ Structured Offers

Instead of combining every promotion into one section, offers were separated into:

* Discount Coupons
* Limited-Time Offers
* Free Delivery
* Buy One Get One

This makes the offers easier to understand and browse.

🛵 Clear Order Tracking

The order tracking experience uses a map and delivery status to provide users with a clearer understanding of where their order is.

🤖 Brew Buddy

I introduced a coffee-themed support assistant to make Help & Support feel more approachable than a traditional FAQ page.

Users can select common issues or ask questions through the support interface.

08. Challenges I Faced

1. Making Figma Variables Work

One of the biggest challenges was understanding how variables could be used to create dynamic interactions.

I experimented with:

* Boolean variables
* String variables
* Conditions
* Component variants
* Dynamic cart states

For example, product customization needed to respond to user selections before allowing an item to be added to the cart.

This helped me understand that Figma can be used to prototype much more than simple screen-to-screen navigation.

---

2. Creating a Dynamic Wishlist

I wanted the wishlist to behave like a real application.

The interaction needed to:

**Tap heart → Add item → Update wishlist → Change heart state**

Removing an item also needed to update the wishlist correctly.

This required careful handling of variables and component states.

---

3. Balancing UI and UX

One of my biggest challenges was trying to make every part of the application feel completely realistic.

Initially, I wanted to create detailed UX flows and interactions for almost every possible situation.

However, I realized that this could significantly increase the project scope and development time.

This taught me an important lesson:

> **A realistic experience doesn't necessarily mean adding more interactions. It means choosing the right interactions for the user.**

Because of this, I prioritized the most important user journeys and focused more strongly on visual design and usability.

---

 4. Designing Many Screens Consistently

The project eventually grew into many different sections — products, cart, wishlist, offers, checkout, tracking, profile, support, and more.

Maintaining consistent:

* Spacing
* Typography
* Components
* Button styles
* Icon usage
* Layouts

across all screens became an important challenge.

This pushed me to think more systematically about UI design.

---

09. What I Learned

This project taught me much more than how to use Figma.

🧠 Design Thinking

I learned to question design decisions instead of simply making screens look attractive.

👤 Understanding User Needs

I started thinking more about what users need to accomplish rather than what I wanted to put on the screen.

📐 Visual Hierarchy

I developed a better understanding of how spacing, typography, size, and placement guide users through an interface.

🧩 Design Systems

Working with reusable components and variants helped me understand the importance of consistency.

🔄 Interactive Prototyping

Variables and conditional interactions helped me create a prototype that behaves more like a real application.

🔍 Self-Critique

One of the most valuable skills I developed was learning to review my own previous work and identify what could be improved.

11. Final Outcome

The redesign helped me move from simply creating screens to thinking more intentionally about the experience behind them.

More importantly, I was able to identify and correct many of the mistakes I made in my first project.

The project also taught me that good UI/UX requires a balance between:

**User needs + usability + visual design + interaction**

rather than focusing on only one of them.

---

12. Key Takeaway

My first coffee app taught me **how to start designing**.

The redesign taught me **how to think about design**.

It reminded me that improvement comes from continuously reviewing your work, identifying problems, experimenting with solutions, and being willing to redesign what you've already created.

And this is only the beginning of my UI/UX journey. ☕🤎
